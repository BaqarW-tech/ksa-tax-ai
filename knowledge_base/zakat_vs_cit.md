# Zakat vs. Corporate Income Tax (CIT)

Saudi Arabia's direct tax system is split by ownership, both administered by ZATCA:

## Zakat
Applies to the Saudi- and GCC-national-owned share of a company's capital. Zakat is a
religious levy calculated on a company's Zakat base (broadly, net worth adjusted for certain
items) rather than on profit alone, and is a distinct concept from VAT or income tax.

## Corporate Income Tax (CIT)
Applies to the foreign-owned share of a company operating in Saudi Arabia, generally at a
20% rate on net adjusted profit attributable to that ownership share.

## Mixed-ownership companies
A company with both Saudi/GCC and foreign shareholders is typically split proportionally:
the Saudi/GCC-owned portion is assessed under Zakat, and the foreign-owned portion under CIT.
Both computations are filed with ZATCA, usually alongside the annual financial statements.

## Withholding Tax (WHT)
Payments made by a Saudi entity to a non-resident (e.g. for services, royalties, or
dividends) may be subject to withholding tax, generally ranging from 5% (e.g. many technical
service fees) up to 20% (e.g. management fees), depending on the payment type and any
applicable double taxation treaty. Correctly classifying a payment (technical service vs.
management fee) matters, since misclassification can trigger tax adjustments and penalties.

## Registration timing
New companies should register for CIT and, where applicable, Zakat promptly after receiving
their Commercial Registration (CR) — commonly advised within 30 days — separately from VAT
registration, which follows its own turnover-based thresholds.

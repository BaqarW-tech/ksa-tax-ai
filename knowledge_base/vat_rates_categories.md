# VAT Rates and Supply Categories

## Standard rate
VAT was introduced in Saudi Arabia on 1 January 2018 at 5%, as part of the GCC Unified VAT
Agreement. The rate was raised to **15%** on 1 July 2020 to support post-pandemic fiscal
revenues, and remains the standard rate.

## Three supply categories
Every taxable supply falls into one of three categories, and getting the classification
right matters for both pricing and input VAT recovery:

1. **Standard-rated (15%)** — most goods and services.
2. **Zero-rated (0%)** — includes qualifying exports, international transport, and certain
   medical/pharmaceutical goods. Zero-rated businesses charge no output VAT but **can still
   reclaim input VAT** on their purchases — this matters a lot for exporters and
   manufacturers with high input costs.
3. **Exempt** — includes certain financial services and residential real estate leasing.
   Exempt supplies also carry 0% output VAT, but unlike zero-rated supplies, the business
   **cannot** reclaim input VAT on related purchases.

## Tourist VAT refunds
Since 18 April 2025, ZATCA operates a Tourist VAT Refund Scheme allowing eligible
international visitors to reclaim VAT on qualifying purchases physically exported from the
Kingdom, subject to a minimum total purchase threshold of SAR 5,000.

## Digital marketplaces
From 1 January 2026, Article 47(3) extends VAT liability to digital marketplace operators
for certain transactions facilitated on their platforms — a change ecommerce and
platform-based businesses should track closely.
